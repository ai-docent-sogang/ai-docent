# Initial Artwork Descriptions (English)

## 1. 정선필 인왕제색도

This is Inwangjesaekdo (Mount Inwang After Rain), painted by Jeong Seon in 1751. Notice the dark, rain-soaked rocks and the pale mist wrapping around the mountainside.

## 2. 씨름, 《단원 풍속도첩》

This is Ssireum (Korean Traditional Wrestling), painted by Kim Hong-do in the late eighteenth century. Can you spot the unusually relaxed sweets vendor among the excited spectators?

## 3. 서당, 《단원 풍속도첩》

This is Seodang (A Village School), painted by Kim Hong-do in the late eighteenth century. One boy is crying, but what expressions do you see on the other children's faces?

## 4. 강세황 초상 자필본

This portrait from 1782 is traditionally identified as a self-portrait of Kang Se-hwang. Isn't it unusual to see a black official hat paired with a scholar's informal robe?

## 5. 김정희 필 세한도

This is Sehando (Winter Scene), painted by Kim Jeong-hui in 1844 during his exile on Jeju Island. Take in the quiet atmosphere created by four trees, a simple house, and generous empty space.

## 6. 끝없이 펼쳐진 강과 산 (이인문 필 강산무진도)

This is Rivers and Mountains without End, painted by Yi In-mun in the early nineteenth century. Let your eyes travel from right to left across the seemingly endless mountains and rivers.

## 7. 김홍도 필 추성부도

This is Chuseongbudo (Sounds of Autumn), painted by Kim Hong-do in 1805. Imagine the sounds of an autumn night as you look at the windblown branches and open-beaked cranes.

## 8. 아기 업은 여인

This is Woman Carrying a Baby on Her Back, painted by Shin Yun-bok in late Joseon. Explore the fashions of the time through her short jeogori jacket, full skirt, and elaborate hairstyle.

## 9. 책가도 6곡병

This is a six-panel Chaekgado folding screen from the late eighteenth century. Don't the books and objects look as though they are sitting on real shelves?

## 10. 어미개와 강아지

This is Painting of a Dog and Puppies, painted by Yi Am in the sixteenth century. Enjoy the tender scene of one puppy sleeping on its mother's back and the others snuggling against her.

## 11. 전 신사임당필 초충도

This is Watermelons and Field Mice from Chochungdo (Plants and Insects), a Joseon work attributed to Shin Saimdang. Can you spot the little mice nibbling at the watermelon and the butterflies fluttering among the flowers?
